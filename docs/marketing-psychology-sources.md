# Grassroots marketing and behavioural design evidence

This is the evidence base used for the public Grassroots welcome experience and product messaging. It is not a claim that every finding transfers unchanged to every Zimbabwean football community. Zimbabwe-specific evidence sets the context; broader behavioural research supplies hypotheses that should still be tested with local users.

## Zimbabwean context

1. [World Bank — Digital Economy for Zimbabwe Country Diagnostic](https://www.worldbank.org/en/country/zimbabwe/publication/digital-transformation-a-key-enabler-of-long-term-resilient-growth-in-zimbabwe)  
   **Design use:** Build for mobile access, uneven connectivity, affordability pressure and a strong mobile-money culture; avoid assuming desktop access.

2. [International Telecommunication Union — Zimbabwe Digital Development Dashboard](https://datahub.itu.int/data/?Connectivity=Use&c=701&e=ZWE)  
   **Design use:** Treat mobile as the primary surface and keep media, forms and navigation light enough for constrained connections.

3. [World Bank — Global Findex 2025, Zimbabwe connectivity questions](https://microdata.worldbank.org/catalog/8001/variable/F1/V176?name=con31b)  
   **Design use:** Data cost is a real adoption barrier, so marketing assets are compressed and the first value proposition is understandable without video.

4. [GSMA — Mobile Gender Gap Report 2024](https://www.gsma.com/solutions-and-impact/connectivity-for-good/mobile-for-development/blog/the-mobile-gender-gap-report-2024/)  
   **Design use:** Make safety, affordability and reliable mobile use visible; do not design a football product around male access patterns alone.

5. [UNICEF Zimbabwe — Adolescents and Youth Strategy 2023–2026](https://www.unicef.org/zimbabwe/reports/adolescents-and-youth-strategy-2023-2026)  
   **Design use:** Emphasise participation, agency, skills, co-creation and age-sensitive safeguarding rather than speaking about young people as passive beneficiaries.

6. [UNICEF Zimbabwe — Child protection](https://www.unicef.org/zimbabwe/child-protection-let-children-grow-safely)  
   **Design use:** Guardian-linked access, hidden sensitive details and clear reporting are core product requirements, not optional trust badges.

7. [UNICEF Zimbabwe — Strengthening protection from online violence](https://www.unicef.org/zimbabwe/stories/strengthening-protection-women-and-children-online-violence)  
   **Design use:** Safety-by-default, clear reporting routes and careful private messaging must be explained in plain language.

8. [UNICEF Zimbabwe — Advocacy briefs on adolescents and young people](https://www.unicef.org/zimbabwe/reports/advocacy-briefs-adolescents-young-people-zimbabwe)  
   **Design use:** Let young users participate in decisions that affect them and avoid a coach-only view of grassroots football.

9. [Doyle et al. — Mental health among adolescents and young adults in Harare and Mashonaland East](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjopen-2022-065276)  
   **Design use:** Use belonging and community connection carefully, without making unsupported mental-health promises.

10. [Manyanga et al. — Physical activity indicators among children and adolescents in Zimbabwe](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jesf.2023.02.001)  
    **Design use:** Present access to regular, enjoyable play as the outcome; do not market only elite pathways.

11. [Manyanga et al. — Zimbabwe’s 2018 Report Card on Physical Activity](https://doi.org/10.1123/jpah.2018-0520)  
    **Design use:** Community environment, family and peers matter alongside organised sport, which supports location-first discovery and guardian involvement.

12. [Dlamini & Grace — Physical activity and socioeconomic status in Zimbabwean adolescents](https://journals.co.za/doi/10.10520/ejc-sport_v44_n1_a1)  
    **Design use:** Avoid messaging that assumes equal facilities, transport, kit or disposable income.

13. [UNICEF Zimbabwe — The case for child and adolescent mental health investment](https://www.unicef.org/zimbabwe/media/9016/file/A%20case%20for%20mental%20health%20of%20children%20and%20adolescents%20in%20Zimbabwe.pdf)  
    **Design use:** Use non-stigmatising, accessible community language and make help and safeguarding routes easy to reach.

14. [Zimbabwe Football Association — Roots Impact Programme](https://zifa.co.zw/zifa-and-bancabc-unite-to-rebuild-football-from-the-roots/)  
    **Design use:** Show transparent pathways from community and school football without pretending the app guarantees professional selection.

15. [Zimbabwe Football Association and Ministry of Education — Grassroots football MOU](https://zifa.co.zw/building-the-future-zifa-and-ministry-of-primary-secondary-education-sign-historic-mou/)  
    **Design use:** Explain football as community development, participation and whole-person growth, not only wins and rankings.

## Sport, well-being and safeguarding

16. [World Health Organization — Guidelines on physical activity and sedentary behaviour](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240014886)  
    **Design use:** Encourage enjoyable and equitable opportunities to play; avoid medicalising the marketing message.

17. [World Health Organization — Adolescent well-being framework](https://www.who.int/activities/promoting-adolescent-well-being)  
    **Design use:** Confidence, secure relationships, support and the resources to participate are part of a healthy football environment.

18. [PMNCH/WHO — Connectedness, positive values and contribution](https://pmnch.who.int/resources/publications/m/item/connectedness-positive-values-and-contributions-to-society-three-building-blocks-of-adolescent-well-being)  
    **Design use:** Frame teams as places to contribute and belong, while keeping physical and emotional safety explicit.

19. [UNICEF — Child protection in digital education](https://www.unicef.org/documents/child-protection-digital-education)  
    **Design use:** Digital opportunity and digital risk must be designed together, especially for youth profiles and communication.

20. [FIFA — Safeguarding and child protection](https://inside.fifa.com/social-impact/safeguarding)  
    **Design use:** Make adult responsibility, reporting and safe participation visible throughout youth football flows.

21. [FIFA — Football for Schools](https://footballforschools.fifa.com/)  
    **Design use:** Football can support learning, life skills and inclusion; marketing should celebrate everyday participation rather than copy professional-club imagery.

22. [Bruner et al. — Sport-based interventions and positive youth development meta-analysis](https://doi.org/10.1080/1750984X.2021.1875496)  
    **Design use:** Positive development is more likely when intentionally supported; merely putting young people into competition is not enough.

23. [Whitley et al. — Sport-based youth development systematic review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6339434/)  
    **Design use:** Relationships, opportunities, support and developmental assets matter, so product success cannot be reduced to fixtures played.

24. [Standage et al. — Autonomy, competence and relatedness in youth physical activity](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.psychsport.2006.12.006)  
    **Design use:** Give users meaningful choice, visible progress and genuine connection instead of coercive prompts.

25. [Banack et al. — Coach autonomy support and athlete motivation](https://doi.org/10.1080/02701367.2011.10599809)  
    **Design use:** Coach language should support player agency and competence, including for disabled participants.

## Behaviour, trust and interface psychology

26. [Ryan & Deci — Self-determination theory](https://doi.org/10.1207/S15327965PLI1104_01)  
    **Design use:** The role choice supports autonomy, the preview builds competence and the community message supports relatedness.

27. [Bandura — Self-efficacy theory](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-295X.84.2.191)  
    **Design use:** Explain the next achievable action and show what success looks like before asking for commitment.

28. [Baumeister & Leary — The need to belong](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.117.3.497)  
    **Design use:** Lead with real team and community belonging, but never exploit fear of exclusion.

29. [Holt-Lunstad et al. — Social relationships and mortality meta-analysis](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pmed.1000316)  
    **Design use:** Treat meaningful social connection as valuable while avoiding health-outcome claims the app cannot prove.

30. [Edmondson — Psychological safety and learning in teams](https://doi.org/10.2307/2666999)  
    **Design use:** Reporting mistakes, disputing a score and raising a concern must feel safe and must not publicly shame a person.

31. [Mayer, Davis & Schoorman — Integrative model of trust](https://doi.org/10.5465/amr.1995.9508080335)  
    **Design use:** Trust comes from ability, benevolence and integrity; profiles and records should show relevant facts instead of claiming “trusted.”

32. [Tyler — Social justice, outcome and procedure](https://doi.org/10.1080/002075900399411)  
    **Design use:** Disputes need voice, neutrality, dignity and a visible process, even when the outcome disappoints one side.

33. [Resnick et al. — Reputation systems](https://doi.org/10.1145/355112.355122)  
    **Design use:** Show behavioural facts such as completed matches, no-shows and response time rather than collapsing reputation into one star score.

34. [Acquisti, Brandimarte & Loewenstein — Privacy and human behaviour](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aaa1465)  
    **Design use:** Do not rely on users to calculate complex privacy trade-offs; protect youth and sensitive information by default.

35. [Michie, van Stralen & West — COM-B and the Behaviour Change Wheel](https://doi.org/10.1186/1748-5908-6-42)  
    **Design use:** A football action needs capability, opportunity and motivation, so prompts alone cannot solve missing grounds, transport or information.

36. [Gollwitzer — Implementation intentions](https://doi.org/10.1037/0003-066X.54.7.493)  
    **Design use:** Turn vague intention into a concrete date, time, place and next action.

37. [Iyengar & Lepper — When choice is demotivating](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.79.6.995)  
    **Design use:** Start with five clear roles and priority filters instead of presenting every feature and every team at once.

38. [Johnson & Goldstein — Default effects](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1091721)  
    **Design use:** Carry agreed availability into a challenge as a transparent, editable default, never as hidden consent.

39. [Nunes & Drèze — The endowed progress effect](https://doi.org/10.1086/500480)  
    **Design use:** A guest preview gives people a truthful sense of progress before account creation, without inventing achievements.

40. [Kahneman & Tversky — Prospect theory](https://doi.org/10.2307/1914185)  
    **Design use:** Avoid manipulative loss language such as “lose your team”; describe what saving an account actually preserves.

41. [Tversky & Kahneman — Framing decisions](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.7455683)  
    **Design use:** Use neutral wording for disputes, cancellation and reliability because framing can change judgement.

42. [Zajonc — Mere exposure](https://doi.org/10.1037/h0025848)  
    **Design use:** Repeat a small, consistent brand and interaction vocabulary rather than constantly introducing new patterns.

43. [Goldstein, Cialdini & Griskevicius — Social norms and pro-environmental behaviour](https://doi.org/10.1086/586910)  
    **Design use:** If social proof is ever shown, it must be local, relevant and true; the marketing page deliberately uses no fabricated user counts.

44. [Freedman & Fraser — Foot-in-the-door effect](https://doi.org/10.1037/h0023552)  
    **Design use:** Let people explore with one reversible role choice before asking for a full profile, while making the later ask explicit.

45. [Fehr & Gächter — Altruistic punishment and cooperation](https://doi.org/10.1038/415137a)  
    **Design use:** Fair enforcement can support cooperation, but penalties need evidence, proportionality and an appeal path.

46. [Sweller — Cognitive load during problem solving](https://doi.org/10.1207/s15516709cog1202_4)  
    **Design use:** Break complex match and tournament work into understandable stages; do not crowd the first screen with administration.

47. [Hick — Rate of information gain and choice](https://doi.org/10.1080/17470215208416600)  
    **Design use:** Prioritise likely actions and progressively reveal less-common choices, especially on a small phone.

48. [Shah, Mullainathan & Shafir — Scarcity and cognitive bandwidth](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1222426)  
    **Design use:** Reduce avoidable decisions and repeated data entry for people managing time, data, transport and money constraints.

49. [Sampson, Raudenbush & Earls — Neighbourhood collective efficacy](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.277.5328.918)  
    **Design use:** Location-first community visibility and shared norms can support coordination; proximity is more useful than national popularity.

50. [Brewer — Optimal distinctiveness and social identity](https://doi.org/10.1177/0146167291175001)  
    **Design use:** Let clubs express a distinct identity while still belonging to a wider local football community.

## Product principles derived from the evidence

- Show value before registration, then ask for the smallest meaningful commitment.
- Put proximity, availability and suitability ahead of popularity.
- Explain outcomes with concrete facts, not fake numbers or vague trust claims.
- Protect young people and sensitive information by default.
- Give both sides voice and visibility in agreements, results and disputes.
- Design for small phones, intermittent connectivity and expensive data.
- Celebrate community football as it exists, including informal grounds and limited facilities.
- Test these hypotheses with Zimbabwean coaches, players, referees, guardians, scouts and sponsors before treating them as settled local truths.
